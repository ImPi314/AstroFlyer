Preliminary formal prior-art confirmation for ECR-MF
Scope and conclusion
This is a preliminary patent-and-literature prior-art assessment , not a legal opinion, claim-construction opinion,
patentability opinion, or freedom-to-operate clearance. The searched concept was ECR-MF: an HEO-specific con-
troller that switches among semi-analytical, regularized, and high-fidelity numerical propagation using predicted
state, event-time, and covariance risk; performs local dual propagation at critical events; escalates fidelity when the
risk budget is exceeded; and emits a machine-checkable certificate.
The search found substantial partial prior art for the ingredients, but not, in the retrieved records, a single reference
that discloses the full combination as narrowly framed for highly eccentric Earth orbits. The strongest prior-art risk
is therefore not that “adaptive propagation” is new; it is that broad claims covering multifidelity orbit propagation,
model selection, error correction, uncertainty ensembles, or event-aware switching may read on existing work.
The defensible position is: ECR-MF may have a patentable or publishable distinction as a specific HEO event-
risk/covariance-aware switching and certification policy, but novelty is not confirmed until the claims are nar-
rowed and a professional search covers claims, continuations, family members, non-patent literature, and rele-
vant CPC classes.
Closest retrieved prior art
1. Non-averaged regularized propagation for HEO
Amato et al. compare semi-analytical and non-averaged regularized propagation, decompose integration error into
dynamical, model-truncation, short-periodic, and numerical components, and test regularized methods on LEO, GTO,
and high-altitude HEO. They report that regularized non-averaged methods can be substantially faster than semi-
analytical methods for the tested HEO case.[1]
ECR-MF implication: regularized/non-averaged propagation and the HEO use case are not new. Do not claim those
elements alone.
2. Adaptive multifidelity uncertainty propagation
Jones and Weisman use low-fidelity propagation to construct a surrogate, selectively re-propagate important samples
with a high-fidelity model, and correct the surrogate for orbit uncertainty propagation.[2] Related multifidelity orbit-
determination work uses a hierarchy of force models and propagation methods and reports a twelve-fold filter-
runtime reduction in the tested case without statistically significant filter-performance change.[3]
ECR-MF implication: broad claims for low/high-fidelity switching, selective high-fidelity samples, uncertainty-
aware acceleration, or orbit-determination runtime reduction are high-risk.
3. Multisegment propagation for highly elliptical orbits
A modified Chebyshev–Picard approach uses nonuniform Chebyshev–Gauss–Lobatto sampling and multiple true-
anomaly-based segments to obtain accurate solutions across eccentric orbits.[4]
ECR-MF implication: segmentation by orbital phase is not new. A claim should require the particular event-risk
and covariance-driven switching rule, not merely true-anomaly segmentation.
4. Verified interval orbit propagation
Verified regularized interval propagation provides mathematically guaranteed position and velocity solutions over
time and is positioned for conjunction analysis and related applications.[5]
ECR-MF implication: machine-verifiable enclosures are not new in isolation. The potential distinction is selective
use of verification at HEO event-risk boundaries, tied to a propagation-mode decision and covariance criterion.

5. Patent: multi-model ensemble atmospheric-density propagation
US20240037297A1 discloses multiple trained density-prediction models, orbit propagation using the respective den-
sity outputs, and ensemble analysis of predicted orbit states to quantify uncertainty and determine an orbit state.[6]
ECR-MF implication: claims covering multiple environmental models, ensemble orbit propagation, or uncertainty-
aware state prediction are exposed. The retrieved disclosure is focused on atmospheric-density model ensembles
rather than HEO event-certified switching among propagation formulations.
6. Patent: physics-plus-machine-learning trajectory correction
US12046129B2 discloses an iterative physics trajectory prediction refined by a machine-learning trajectory-error
model, with a pseudo-measurement and pseudo-measurement noise used to update the future trajectory.[7]
ECR-MF implication: claims covering a physics baseline plus learned error correction, pseudo-measurements, or
model-error-based trajectory updates are exposed. ECR-MF should not require machine learning unless the specific
trained error model is itself the intended contribution.
7. Patent: transformer-based orbital prediction
WO2025065111A1 discloses transformer-based resident-space-object trajectory prediction using state vectors and
Keplerian data, including a forecast horizon and recursive use of predictions.[8]
ECR-MF implication: generic claims to AI-based orbital prediction are exposed and should be avoided. This reference
does not, from the retrieved summary, disclose HEO event-risk switching, high-fidelity certification, or covariance-
gated mode selection.
8. Patent: distributed local orbit propagation
US7612712B2 discloses a server/client architecture in which a server distributes seed state vectors and propagation-
model coefficients so a client can locally propagate satellite orbits.[9]
ECR-MF implication: distributed deployment and local propagation are not new. They should be treated as imple-
mentation context, not the inventive core.

Recommended claim strategy
Avoid an independent claim framed as “an adaptive multifidelity orbit propagator. ” That is too broad and directly
exposed by the multifidelity literature and related patents.
A stronger independent method claim should require all of the following as a linked sequence:
1. receiving a highly eccentric Earth-orbit state, force-model configuration, covariance, and mission event thresh-
olds;
2. propagating a first segment using a selected low-cost or regularized method;
3. predicting both state error and event-time/covariance risk for a future perigee, maneuver, eclipse, apogee, or
ground-contact event;
4. initiating a second, independent certification propagation only when the predicted risk reaches a defined
boundary;
5. comparing state, event-time, and covariance outputs from the two methods;
6. accepting the low-cost propagation only when all three error conditions remain within budget, otherwise
rolling back or escalating fidelity; and
7. emitting a machine-readable certificate recording the selected mode, force model, event bound, covariance test,
and escalation decision.
The claim should specify how the event-time bound is computed, how covariance inconsistency is detected, what
triggers rollback, and how the certified state is transferred between formulations. Those details—not the labels
“adaptive, ” “multifidelity, ” “regularized, ” or “AI”—are where technical distinction is most likely to reside.
Confirmation status
Preliminary confirmation: partial prior art found; full-combination anticipation not found in the retrieved set.
The ECR-MF concept is not cleanly clear of prior art. Its broad ingredients are substantially occupied, while the nar-
rower combination of HEO event-risk prediction, covariance-aware mode switching, local dual certification, rollback,
and machine-readable proof remains a plausible research and patent angle.
Before filing or making a definitive novelty statement, perform:
