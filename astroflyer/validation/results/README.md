How we got here (each fix written test-first):

- **Shadow edges.** SRP has a kink at penumbra/umbra entry and exit. DOP853 stepping across
  it cost 7.5 m/day on Molniya; the propagator now restarts at each edge (backend/eclipse.py).
- **Local solar time.** Orekit's NRLMSISE00 takes local time from the true Sun; MSIS (and
  AstroFlyer) use UT + longitude/15. The equation of time made ~0.3% in density (30 m/day on
  GTO). The reference now feeds Orekit a mean Sun, so both follow the MSIS convention.
- **Atmosphere precision.** pymsis runs NRLMSISE-00 in single precision; its ~1e-6 density
  jitter made GTO drag results differ by metres between computers (the laptop showed 238 m
  over 7 days on GTO L7). AstroFlyer now uses a double-precision port (backend/nrlmsise00.py,
  bit-identical to the public-domain C release) and restarts where the model itself jumps
  (fixed altitudes such as 160/300/450 km, and UTC midnight).
- **Relativity** agrees with Orekit to millimetres.

- **Earth's shape in the shadow.** Orekit casts the shadow with the WGS-84 ellipsoid; AstroFlyer
  used a sphere of equatorial radius. Seen from a Molniya or Tundra apogee the limb toward the
  Sun can lie near a pole, 21 km lower, so our penumbra was too dark. AstroFlyer now finds the ellipsoid limb in the same plane as
  Orekit (`forces.occulting_radius`); the penumbra fraction agrees with Orekit to 1e-10.
  Over 7 days Molniya L5 went from 31.7 m to 1.7 m and Tundra L5 from 2.3 m to 2.9 cm.
  (A cloud run had shown 0.87 m for Molniya L5; the tables here are the laptop run.)
