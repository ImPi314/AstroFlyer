# Cross-check results

AstroFlyer against three independent propagators, Orekit, NASA GMAT and Basilisk, on the same cases.

## Orekit

Generated by `python -m astroflyer.validation.orekit_crosscheck` (Orekit 13.1 via orekit-jpype),
epoch 2026-01-01 00:00 TT, GCRF. Both sides start from the same Cartesian state and use the
same EGM2008 coefficients, spacecraft (Cr·A/m 0.02, Cd·A/m 0.01 m²/kg) and constant space
weather (F10.7 = F10.7a = 150, Ap = 15). Orekit uses its own DE440 Sun/Moon and NRLMSISE-00.

### Full force-model ladder (laptop run after the WGS-84 shadow fix, all 52 cases pass)

| Run | Levels | Limit | Largest difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [1 day](full_1day/orekit_crosscheck.md) | L0–L7, all four orbits | 10 m | 0.63 m (GTO L6) |
| [7 days](full_7day/orekit_crosscheck.md) | L3–L7, all four orbits | 100 m | 6.1 m (GTO L7) |

Selected 7-day differences: Molniya L5 (SRP) 1.7 m, Molniya L7 5.2 m, GTO L6 (drag) 2.9 m,
Tundra L7 2.1 cm, stress orbit L7 4.5 m.

How we got here (each fix written test-first):

- **Shadow edges.** SRP has a kink at penumbra/umbra entry and exit. DOP853 stepping across
  it cost 7.5 m/day on Molniya; the propagator now restarts at each edge (backend/eclipse.py).
- **Local solar time.** Orekit's NRLMSISE00 takes local time from the true Sun; MSIS (and
  AstroFlyer) use UT + longitude/15. The equation of time made ~0.3% in density (30 m/day on
  GTO). The reference now feeds Orekit a mean Sun, so both follow the MSIS convention.
- **Atmosphere precision.** pymsis runs NRLMSISE-00 in single precision; its ~1e-6 density
  jitter made GTO drag results differ by metres between computers (the laptop showed 238 m
  over 7 days on GTO L7). AstroFlyer now uses a double-precision port (backend/nrlmsise00.py,
  bit-identical to the public-domain C release) and restarts where the model itself jumps
  (fixed altitudes such as 160/300/450 km, and UTC midnight).
- **Relativity** agrees with Orekit to millimetres.

- **Earth's shape in the shadow.** Orekit casts the shadow with the WGS-84 ellipsoid; AstroFlyer
  used a sphere of equatorial radius. Seen from a Molniya or Tundra apogee the limb toward the
  Sun can lie near a pole, 21 km lower, so our penumbra was too dark. AstroFlyer now finds the ellipsoid limb in the same plane as
  Orekit (`forces.occulting_radius`); the penumbra fraction agrees with Orekit to 1e-10.
  Over 7 days Molniya L5 went from 31.7 m to 1.7 m and Tundra L5 from 2.3 m to 2.9 cm.
  (A cloud run had shown 0.87 m for Molniya L5; the tables here are the laptop run.)

## GMAT

Generated by `python -m astroflyer.validation.gmat_crosscheck` with NASA GMAT R2026a (Apache 2.0),
same cases, levels, epoch and limits as the Orekit runs, output every 600 s. GMAT gets AstroFlyer's
EGM2008 coefficients (written as a .cof file), the same spacecraft (Cd·A/m 0.01, Cr·A/m 0.02 with
the solar flux set so Flux/c is AstroFlyer's 4.56e-6 N/m²), constant space weather (F10.7 150,
Kp 3 = Ap 15) and its built-in EarthICRF frame (GCRF), propagating with RungeKutta89 at 1e-13.

| Run | Levels | Limit | Largest difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [1 day](gmat_1day/gmat_crosscheck.md) | L0–L7, all four orbits | 10 m | 1.06 m (Molniya L5) |
| [7 days](gmat_7day/gmat_crosscheck.md) | L3–L7, all four orbits | 100 m | 32.6 m (Molniya L5) |

All 52 cases pass. Through L4 (gravity, Sun and Moon) every orbit agrees to 4–7 cm over a day; over
7 days the gravity-only differences (0.87 m Molniya, 1.88 m GTO) are the same as against Orekit,
i.e. AstroFlyer's own integration error at rtol 1e-12.

Where the larger differences come from, each confirmed by an experiment that changed AstroFlyer to
match GMAT and watched the difference vanish:

- **Shadow shape (L5).** GMAT casts the Earth's shadow from a sphere; AstroFlyer, like Orekit, uses
  the WGS-84 ellipsoid. With a spherical shadow AstroFlyer matches GMAT at L5 to 0.043 m (Molniya)
  and 0.039 m (Tundra) over a day, instead of 1.06 m and 0.15 m. Over 7 days this is Molniya's
  32.6 m, the same size as AstroFlyer showed against Orekit before it moved to the ellipsoid.
- **Drag density (L6).** GMAT's NRLMSISE-00 returns the total mass density without anomalous
  oxygen (`gtd7`); AstroFlyer uses `gtd7d`, which the model's authors recommend for drag and which
  Orekit's results agree with (gtd7 versus gtd7d moves Molniya L6 by 1.1 m; Orekit and AstroFlyer differ there by 0.15 m). Anomalous oxygen matters above ~500 km, so GTO (250 km perigee) barely notices
  (drag moves it 9.2 km a day; the two agree on that to 0.048 m) while Molniya's 538 km perigee does
  (1.1 m a day). With `gtd7` AstroFlyer matches GMAT's drag to 0.033 m (Molniya) and 0.013 m (GTO).
  At L6–L7 this partly offsets the shadow difference on Molniya, so 16 m there is not closer
  agreement, just two differences of opposite sign.

GMAT reads Sun and Moon from its own DE424 file: when these runs were made SPICE refused AstroFlyer's
bundled DE440 subset (the file's last 1024-byte record was short; jplephem, which AstroFlyer uses, read
it; padded to whole records on 2026-10-26). DE424 and DE440 differ by metres at most for the Sun
and Moon in 2026, about 1e-13 m/s² at the spacecraft.

To reproduce (GMAT installed; set GMAT_CONSOLE to GmatConsole.exe if it is not found):

    python -m astroflyer.validation.gmat_crosscheck --days 1 --out astroflyer/validation/results/gmat_1day
    python -m astroflyer.validation.gmat_crosscheck --days 7 --levels L3 L4 L5 L6 L7 --out astroflyer/validation/results/gmat_7day

## Basilisk

Generated by `python -m astroflyer.validation.basilisk_crosscheck` with Basilisk 2.11.1 (AVS Lab,
University of Colorado; ISC licence), same cases, epoch and limits, output every 600 s. Basilisk runs
in its own virtual environment (`~/bsk-venv`, or set BSK_PYTHON) as a separate process
(`basilisk_runner.py`), integrating with RKF78 at rtol 1e-12. It gets AstroFlyer's EGM2008
coefficients, constants, spacecraft and space weather; Sun, Moon and Earth orientation come from the
SPICE kernels shipped with GMAT (DE424, ITRF93 high-precision PCK). Basilisk has no relativity, so
L7 is not run. The SRP area is scaled so Basilisk's 1361 W/m² solar flux gives AstroFlyer's
4.56e-6 N/m².

| Run | Levels | Limit | Largest difference | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [1 day](basilisk_1day/basilisk_crosscheck.md) | L0–L6, all four orbits | 10 m | 0.72 m through L5 (Molniya L5); L6 313 m (GTO) | 26/28 pass |
| [7 days](basilisk_7day/basilisk_crosscheck.md) | L3–L6, all four orbits | 100 m | 30.3 m through L5 (Molniya L5); L6 16.6 km (GTO) | 14/16 pass |

Three Basilisk behaviours are corrected in the runner, because they are numerical artefacts rather
than modelling choices:

- **Earth orientation within a task step.** Basilisk's gravity steps the Earth's orientation matrix
  linearly inside each task step (`gravityEffector.cpp`, `dcm += dcm_dot * dt`), so the matrix stops
  being a rotation and scales even two-body gravity by about (ωΔt)²/2: 204 m on Molniya over 6 h with
  a 60 s step. The runner holds the orientation at mid-step, re-orthonormalised (`HeldOrientation`);
  two-body then agrees to 1.9 cm a day.
- **Air co-rotation rate.** The rate `zeroWindModel` derives from SPICE made GTO drag 9% too strong;
  the runner sets the IERS rate about +z.
- **Environment held over a task step.** Eclipse, atmosphere and wind are evaluated once per task
  step, from the state at the step's start, and held while the spacecraft integrates across it.
  The lag is first order: with AstroFlyer on spherical altitude, GTO drag differed by 23.8 m a day at
  a 1 s step, 12 m at 0.5 s, 6.6 m at 0.25 s and 3.2 m at 0.1 s. The runner hands these modules the
  state moved to mid-step, r + v·Δt/2 (`MidStepState`); at 1 s the difference is then 0.51 m.

SRP and drag levels run with a 1 s task step, gravity-only levels with 10 s.

What is left:

- **Gravity, Sun and Moon (L0–L4)** agree to 2 cm (two-body) and 0.37 m (with J2 or 8×8) over a
  day. The 0.37 m on Molniya is not explained. Checked on 2026-10-26 and ruled out: the gravity field
  (Basilisk's own `computeField` on the same file matches AstroFlyer's J2 acceleration to 1e-12
  relative), Earth orientation (SPICE ITRF93 from the high-precision PCK and AstroFlyer's IAU 2006/2000A
  ITRF differ by 0.009" over the day, pole included), GM and reference radius (identical), and
  integration (the same 0.3721 m with a 1 s or 10 s task step and with tolerances from 1e-10 to 1e-13).
  The difference grows steadily from the start (0.02 m after 6 h), as from a slightly different
  period; Orekit and GMAT agree with AstroFlyer on the same case to 0.008 m and 0.066 m.
- **SRP (L5)** agrees to 0.72 m a day and 30.3 m over 7 days on Molniya. Basilisk's shadow is a
  sphere of equatorial radius, like GMAT's, which gave GMAT the same Molniya difference (32.6 m).
- **Drag (L6) fails on Molniya and GTO.** Basilisk's NRLMSISE-00 takes spherical altitude and
  geocentric latitude, not WGS-84 geodetic. With AstroFlyer switched to spherical altitude in an
  experiment the 1-day difference falls from 313 m to 0.51 m (GTO) and from 33 m to 0.10 m
  (Molniya); what is left is mostly Basilisk spinning the air about GCRF z rather than the true
  pole (0.31 m and 0.06 m with that matched too). The 7-day figures are the same difference
  growing along track. Tundra and the stress orbit, whose perigees sit higher, pass.

To reproduce (Basilisk installed in ~/bsk-venv, GMAT installed for the kernels):

    python -m astroflyer.validation.basilisk_crosscheck --days 1 --out astroflyer/validation/results/basilisk_1day
    python -m astroflyer.validation.basilisk_crosscheck --days 7 --levels L3 L4 L5 L6 --out astroflyer/validation/results/basilisk_7day
